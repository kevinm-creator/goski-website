Why ski tracker numbers differ between apps

Ski the same day with two tracker apps and you can get two different answers. Here’s why, and what to look for.

Updated October 24, 2026

“Vertical” doesn’t always mean the same thing

Some apps count only the downhill on your runs. Others add every descent, including walking and lift rides down. Neither is wrong, but they won’t match. Look for a tracker that says plainly what it counts.

GPS knows where you are better than how high

Phones pin down where you are far better than how high you are. Height from GPS alone can drift by tens of feet, especially under trees, beside buildings and right after you start, and small errors add up over a day.

A barometer helps

Most iPhones have a barometer, and air pressure changes smoothly as you go up and down the mountain. Trackers that use it tend to give steadier vertical than GPS height alone.

Lift rides shouldn’t count as runs

A chair going up is easy to spot. Flat lifts, mid-stations and gondolas ridden back down are harder, and a tracker that misses them can add fake runs or extra vertical.

Naming runs needs a real trail map

Any tracker can count runs. Telling you which trails you skied takes a detailed trail map of the resort.

Signal and privacy

Recording should never depend on cell service, which is patchy on most mountains. And a ski day is a precise record of where you’ve been, so it’s worth checking where your recordings are kept.
